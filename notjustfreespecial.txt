## Non-negotiable completion rules

A mechanic may be marked complete only when all of the following are true:

- Real source content exists and is converted correctly.
- The packed data preserves what the runtime needs.
- The runtime implements the actual relevant OpenBOR behavior, not merely animation selection.
- A live gate proves the trigger, behavior, effect, completion and important negative/regression cases.
- The gate uses independent ground truth wherever practical.
- Documentation describes the actual implementation without hiding simplifications.

The following do not count as completion:

- Assigning a mechanic to another gate.
- Playing its animation through a made-up button combination.
- Checking only `CHAR_SELECTED`, `CHAR_ATTACK_VARIANT` or frame-count metadata.
- Calling a one-shot attack “grab” without a real link and hold.
- Calling a mechanic content-inapplicable without proving both content configuration and source reachability.
- Declaring something out of scope after previous documentation claimed the phase exhausted the real moveset.
- A synthetic WRAM setup without a corresponding live behavioral check where the mechanic is supposed to be naturally reachable.

Direct WRAM writes are acceptable for controlled setup and isolation, but they must be clearly identified as setup. They cannot replace the live trigger or the behavior under test.

Do not introduce another stand-in trigger or approximation and then label it as real OpenBOR behavior. A deliberate simplification may remain only when it is explicitly documented as partial and does not support a “phase complete” claim.

Do not stop after finding a gap. Fix it and add a regression gate unless it is genuinely content-inapplicable or externally blocked. When declaring something inapplicable, record the exact raw-content search, relevant OpenBOR source gate, and why no shipped configuration can reach it.
